## 1. Game pitch
A low-poly, three.js life-and-work sim across six districts (Oak Street / Westside city, Alder Center, Cedar Heights, Eastbank Works, Willow Fields, Pine Ridge). The player meets needs (food, thirst, energy), earns money through gigs and trades (delivery, logging and milling, store jobs, farming), rents or buys a home, furnishes it, and climbs skill ladders toward owning a shop or running logistics. Offline play first; multiplayer is an opt-in relay.

## 3. Architecture rules
- **Coordinates:** 1 unit = 1 m, +Y up, **north is −z**, player 1.95 m, spawn (−5, 15).
- **Flat world in v1, elevation later.** No elevation yet; ridges and river are scenery/barriers. To avoid a rewrite later: all ground height goes through one function `heightAt(x, z)` (returns 0 today) and the player, vehicles, trees, plots and props read it instead of assuming y = 0; a test fails if gameplay code hardcodes ground height; saves store x and z only and recompute y on load (interior floors keep their own heights); plots and building footprints carry a flat-pad flag in data so terrain can be carved around them later. Vehicles (kinematic) follow `heightAt` and tilt to the slope; walking and driving get a maximum slope when elevation arrives. `heightAt` and its test ship with 0.33.
- **Data first.** World lives in `dist/map-definition.js` (zones, road graph, fields, buildings, shops, plots, scenery params), checked by `validateMap`. New districts = data + scenery code that reads it.
- **Terrain mask** (`terrain.js`, 0.25.0): 0.5 m cells from zone shapes, road ribbons along centrelines and water; used for walking, footing, scenery exclusion, minimap. `validateTerrain` proves every district is reachable on foot from spawn.
- **Saves.** One plain object `citylife.progress.v1` (localStorage), rebuilt by `normalizeProgress` (jobs.js). `progress.schema` (missing = 1). Adding fields needs no bump; changing a field's meaning bumps the schema with one migration step (`if (schema < N)`), each with a fixture test (`tests/fixtures/save-schema-N.json`). Schema 2 (adds `properties`) is done; schema 3 (tree `hits` → `damage` = hits x 8; tools get a condition) ships with 0.37.
- **Changes** go through pure transaction functions (`transactJob`, `housingTransaction`, `lifeTransaction`): state + action in, new state or error out. Money is integer cents.
- **Every placement** goes through `placeSafely` (reset, teleport, unstick, load, stuck rescue). New flat ground meshes go through `groundLayer()` (`GROUND_LAYERS`). Generated trees stay 2.5 m off landmarks (`nearProp`).
- **Registry rule:** everything new is enterable unless `MAP.properties` says otherwise (a test, not a convention).
- **Dev mode** is one removable flag (`dev-flag.js`, `dev.js`); nothing of it in `index.html` or `style.css`.
- **Anti-exploit rule:** every job or gig gets its own anti-exploit tuning when built (daily cap, falling price, cooldown or similar). Not designed in advance.
- **Assets.** 1 unit = 1 m, character 1.95 m, +Z forward, Y up, pivot at base centre, flat-shaded look; `ASSETS.md` + manifest (maps reference ids like `vehicle.pickup`, not filenames). Scales: Kenney Furniture Kit 2 m/unit, Quaternius interior pack 0.5 m/unit, Kenney houses/commercial 8 m/unit. Check each pack's licence. Real assets over fake ones; say what is missing instead of faking it.
- **Physics:** no rigid-body engine. Vehicles are kinematic arcade (decided): acceleration and braking ramps, speed-based turning, visual body tilt, heavier loaded trucks; tested for collisions and road footing, not simulated suspension or skidding. Scripted animation only; if tumbling objects are ever wanted, cap ~20 active bodies near the player that freeze when still.
- **River crossings (decided):** the river is crossable only at bridges (County Bridge, the second bridge at x 190..200 on the Freight Road) for players, NPCs and vehicles.
- **Multiplayer today:** relay (`server.js`) forwarding positions and a `room` (`street`, `market`, `cafe`, `home`, plus `^house:[a-z0-9-]{1,40}$`). Nothing is server-authoritative; no new feature may assume shared persistent state until that changes.

### Performance budget (flag with a number and a cheaper alternative before building anything that would exceed)
- More than ~1,000 tree-sized instances (1,000 trees ≈ 1.2 M triangles; choppable ones cost double for shadows) or large grass/rock/flower scatter (a few thousand within 30 m, chunked).
- Crops one mesh per cell (use chunked low-poly instances, within 90 m, cap 150,000 crop triangles).
- More than ~15 skinned characters or ~10 cars on screen (vehicle design cap: 15 drawn).
- Bulk shadow casters, textures above 512 px per asset, more than ~300 draw calls.
- Measured: all trees 0.89 M triangles; culling draws 5-60% depending on where you stand; ~45-100 draw calls. Claude cannot measure real FPS (software renderer, ~4 FPS); use triangle/draw-call counts plus the user's reports (about 165 FPS on a real GPU; 105 at the bridge before culling).
- Levers in place: fog and cull distance, no-shadow scenery, instancing. Next: chunked far versions, lower-poly LODs.

### 4.7 Carry system — Status: Planned (0.34)
- **Items:** a log is carried **one at a time on the shoulder**; planks are carried **3-4 at a time** the same way (reference image: `docs/concept-logging.jpg`). Carrying animations and rules ship in 0.34.
- **Movement:** slower walking while carrying, no sprint: 80% of walk speed with a log, 85% with planks, stamina cost 1.5x and 1.25x *(built; tune after playtesting)*.
- **Plank stack size (built in 0.32.x, one size everywhere):** a plank is **1.2 m long and 0.45 m across** (the model's 2.4 m plank shrunk about its middle; thickness unchanged), four to a stack, 12 cm per board. The same plank is on the cutting clip, the belt, the ground and in the hands. A stack put down on the ground is a solid box of 1.2 x 0.45 m (0.45 x 1.2 m after a quarter turn; the turn is a multiple of 90 degrees and saved as q), at most 12 stacks, which never expire. A stack is carried crosswise at chest height (1.20 to 1.66 m up, back edge 0.28 m in front of the body) with both hands under it; a log rides on the right shoulder with its axis at 1.40 m, top 1.52 m, below the chin, and the right hand holds it up from underneath. Picking up and putting down are one bend: 50 degree spine lean, 0.22 m hip drop, the item changes hands at the bottom.
- **Rules:** carrying blocks every other action (`actions.js`); doors prompt "put it down first"; a drop key puts the item on the ground as a world item so the player is never stuck.
- **Save:** `progress.carrying = {item, count}`, restored on load. Loose world items are saved as counts or positions per the existing plot pattern; never one record per plank in a pile.
- Also used later for furniture boxes and stock boxes.

### 4.8 Tools, logging, sawmill, fuel, repairs — Status: one axe today; bucking, carry and sawmill 0.34; fuel 0.35; tool ladder, wear, chainsaws and repairs 0.37
**The chain (logs and planks are not sold; the sawmill adds the value):**
fell tree (axe or chainsaw) → the **trunk** stays on the ground → **buck** it into logs (Hand Saw or chainsaw, one log per cut) → carry each log to the sawmill → sawmill turns 1 log into 4 planks *(placeholder)* over the blade's cut time → planks pile beside the sawmill (instanced, capped draw; count saved) → load a vehicle (0.35) → haul to Eastbank Works → sell planks (0.35). In 0.34 planks can be milled and piled but not sold; use a dev-mode grant or sale to test the economy.
- **Bucking (required).** Axes fell only; they cannot buck. A tree takes 3-4 cuts (logs: a third log at lumberjack L3, a fourth at L8, plus the Lumberjack Axe's occasional bonus log). **Hand Saw** (in 0.34): 4 s per cut (12-16 s a tree), $15, unlock level 1, sold at the hardware counter *(placeholder)*; it wears like other tools once wear ships in 0.37 (~$0.20 per tree, placeholder). **Chainsaw** (0.37): 1 s per cut, with a small fuel use per cut. An unbucked trunk disappears after about 1 game day *(placeholder)* and adds no regrowth block beyond the existing stump rule.
- **Tree plot save:** `{tree, damage, felledAt, plantedAt, trunkLogs}` where `trunkLogs` is the count of logs left to cut from the trunk. Logs on the ground are world items.
- **Plank price: $1.00 each** *(placeholder)*, so a tree of 12-16 planks is worth $12-16. Eastbank buys at this price; later players may work at or own Eastbank and set prices. The Reforestation premium (below) applies to the plank price.
- **Saw blades** (spin at the bench and wear; wear counts planks cut; spares sold at the Eastbank supplier) *(placeholders)*:

| Blade | Price | Lasts | Cost per plank | Cut time per log |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Standard | $25 | 300 planks | ~$0.083 | 6 s |
| Upgraded | $40 | 600 planks | ~$0.067 | 4.5 s |
| High-End | $100 | 1,800 planks | ~$0.056 | 3 s |

- **Sawmill build needs (0.34):** a split blade mesh or overlay disc that spins, an audio system (none yet: Web Audio or CC0 files), the plank item and stack save format (Opus designs).
- **Tools model (0.37):** `tools.js` table `{id, model, damage, swingSeconds, durabilityHits, unlockLevel, price, fuel?:{tankMl,mlPerSecond}}`. Swing damage = tool damage × (1 + 0.04 × (level − 1)); hits = ceil(toughness / damage); pine toughness 24. `chopTree` reads only the equipped hotbar tool.
- **Ladder** (hits for a pine L1 / L20): Axe L1 $20 dmg 3 (8/5); Steel Axe L5 $120 dmg 4 (6/4); Lumberjack Axe L10 $350 dmg 6 (4/3, two-handed, bonus log). **Chainsaws (low and high):** low chainsaw $600, lumberjack L15, cheap, ~2× fuel, wears fast *(placeholder)*; high chainsaw (SKU0374) $1,500, lumberjack L20, ~1.8 s per pine, half the fuel, wears slowly *(placeholder)*. Hold E to cut.
- **Wear (decided: yes; 0.37).** Condition 0-1000 per tool instance (bag slot `{id, quantity:1, cond, fuel?}`); falls by 1000/durabilityHits per hit; dull tool damage × (0.6 + 0.4 × condition); at 0 it is broken, not destroyed. Repairs: whetstone (+25%, cheap) and workshop (100% for ~40% of price), sold at the Eastbank supplier. Per-tree wear: Axe $0.50, Steel $0.30, Lumberjack $0.25, low chainsaw ~$1.00 + $0.40 fuel, high ~$0.50 + $0.20 fuel.
- **Wear tax per tree** (tool wear + fuel + blade, against $12-16 of planks; targets 15-30% low end, 5-10% high end), before adding Hand Saw wear and chainsaw bucking fuel: Axe + Standard blade ~$1.50-1.85 (~11-13%); low chainsaw + Standard blade ~$2.40-2.75 (~17-20%); high chainsaw + High-End blade ~$1.40-1.60 (~9-11%). Retune once bucking costs are in.
- **Fuel (0.35):** priced at the gas station, **gas $4.00/gal, diesel $4.20/gal** (imperial, whole cents); the Gas Can is a $15 tool that holds 5 gal of one fuel (`fuelMgal`/`fuelType` on its bag slot; hold F at a pump to fill it, at a tank cap to pour it in). A chainsaw would burn roughly 0.05 gal a tree (felling; bucking extra). The fuel system (pump, tank, can) ships with 0.35 for vehicles; chainsaws reuse it in 0.37. In 0.33 the pumps are props only. Stations: one by the woods road, one on the Freight Road. Low-fuel warning, jerry can, paid tow as last resort. Later, an owned station may set its own price.
- **Power Drill:** Handyman skill tool (props first, then jobs).
- **Regrowth:** today stump → sapling after 3 game days → pine after 7 (1 game day = 48 real minutes); forest empties in ~90 min of nonstop chopping. Proposed: sapling 1 day, pine 2.5 days, stump blocks until cleared (3 swings) or 4 days; pine cones (~1 in 3) plant saplings (mature in 2 days) with a Reforestation plank-price premium while forest > 70%; target: replanters stay > 60%. Add oak and giant tree later. XP: 2 per swing, fall XP scales with toughness; retune so L15 ≈ 150 pines (~40 min). Today: XP 40 to L2, +20 per level, cap 20.
- **Lumberjack contracts** (lumber yard) reuse the gig system: deliver N planks.
- **Models:** Opus (carry rules, bucking and trunk save, plank item, stack save, numbers); Sonnet (`tools.js`, wear, fuel, HUD bar, sawmill, bucking, carry animation); Haiku (prices, durability).

#### D. Driving (kinematic arcade; Stage 3)
**Files.** `dist/vehicle.js` (pure, tested): classes, `stepVehicle(state, input, dt, env)`, hull and collision maths. `dist/driving.js`: meshes, enter/exit, camera, HUD prompt, wired into `game.js` by a few lines (stepWorld, frame, interactions, blocked()). Map data: `MAP.vehicles = [{id, model, home:{x, z, yaw}}]`: **`eastbank-pickup`** and **`eastbank-flatbed`** in the Eastbank Depot (flatbed in truck bay 1 at (86.5, −13), pickup in car bay 1 at (94.35, −15.5), both nose north, yaw π). The farm's pickup and the timber yard's flatbed stay parked scenery (decor, not drivable). No ownership or keys in 0.33: anyone can drive the depot vehicles.

**State** `{id, model, x, z, yaw, speed, steer}` (y is never stored: `y = heightAt(x, z) + FOOTING[surface]`, smoothed like the player's feet). Forward is the model's +z.
**Classes** *(placeholders)*:

| Class (model) | Top speed m/s | Reverse | Accel m/s² | Brake | Coast drag | Min turn radius | Max lateral accel |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| everyday car (later: `car`, `suv`...) | 11 | 4 | 3.0 | 7 | 1.0 | 5.5 m | 5.0 |
| van (later) | 10 | 3.5 | 2.4 | 6 | 0.9 | 6.5 m | 4.5 |
| **pickup** (`pickup`) | **11** (everyday) | 4 | 2.8 | 7 | 1.0 | 6 m | 5.0 |
| **flatbed** (`flatbed`) | **10 empty, 8 loaded** (load 0..1 from cargo in 0.35; always 0 in 0.33) | 3 | 1.8 x (1 − 0.3 x load) | 5 | 0.8 | 9 m | 3.5 |

- **Turning:** `R(v) = max(Rmin, v² / aLat)`, `yawRate = steer x v / R(v)` (sign of v, so reversing steers correctly). Steering input ramps to full in 0.4 s and returns to centre in 0.3 s. At 11 m/s the pickup turns no tighter than 24 m; the Cedar loop's 20 m bends need about 10 m/s, so you ease off a little.
- **Surfaces** (top speed and accel multiplied by): asphalt, street, road, bridge, driveway 1.0; dirt 0.8; **grass, fields, pavement, path 0.5** ("grass at half speed"); **water and bank: never** (treated as solid). Off the playable rectangle: solid.
- **Controls:** W throttle; S brakes while moving forward and reverses once below 0.3 m/s; A/D steer; Space brake (x1.5); E get out. Mouse orbits the camera, which springs back behind the vehicle after 1.5 s. Hotbar, chop and sprint are off while driving; phone, inventory and Esc work (the simulation pauses as now, so the vehicle stops in place).
- **Visual tilt** only: pitch = −0.01 x accel (±0.04 rad), roll = 0.012 x lateral accel (±0.05 rad). The flatbed's generated wheels (`vehicle-parts.js`, 6 instanced tyres and hubs) spin by speed / 0.75 m and the front pair steers; the pickup's wheels are baked into its mesh, so they do not turn (accepted; a split wheel mesh is a converter job for later).
- **Collisions (existing collider system, no new engine).** The hull is an oriented box from the converted model's footprint: pickup 2.27 x 5.3 m, flatbed 4.2 x 8.6 m (**check:** 4.2 m is wide for a flatbed; if it is the mirrors or a bed overhang, a two-box hull from its converter colliders, bed and cab, is used instead). A pose is free when every **hull sample point** passes the game's own `blocked()`: points every 0.5 m along the perimeter, **inset by the player radius 0.33 m**, so `blocked()`'s 0.33 m inflation lands exactly on the hull edge (a 0.16 m sign post cannot slip between samples: the gap must be under 2 x 0.49 m). Plus the surface rule above. Moving: try the full step; if blocked, try x only, then z only (sliding along walls like the player); if all fail, keep the old pose, speed 0, and refuse the yaw change too. **Other drivable vehicles:** exact oriented-box overlap (SAT). **The player and NPCs vs vehicles:** `blocked()` gains one test, a point inside any drivable vehicle's oriented box inflated by 0.33 m; pedestrians block a vehicle (it stops) and are never knocked over. Remote players do not collide (positions only). Trees (trunk circles), fences, buildings, parked cars, lamps and the new yard props are all already in `blocked()`.
- **Never stuck:** loading a save or ending up in a blocked pose (a rule change, a prop placed later) runs `placeVehicleSafely`: the nearest clear pose by the same spiral as `safe-spot.js` (yaw kept, then +90°, +180°, +270°), else the vehicle's home bay. Event-driven, like `placeSafely`.
- **Enter:** "E Drive the pickup" is an interaction candidate within 2.0 m of the **driver's door point** (the vehicle's left side, local (+hx + 0.6, door z)). Refused, with the reason as a toast, when: carrying a log or planks ("Put it down first", the doors' rule; `carryEffects`/`carry` is only read); mid-bend (`putting`), chopping, consuming or any busy action (`interact()` already returns early); inside a shop or house. An equipped tool stays equipped in the save and is hidden while driving. Entering hides the avatar (no seated pose in the cab in 0.33: the cab has no interior and a body would clip it).
- **Exit:** E while below 1 m/s ("Stop first" otherwise). You appear at the driver's door point; if that is blocked, the passenger side, then behind, then in front; then `placeSafely` from the door point. Exiting never puts you inside the hull (it is part of `blocked()`).
- **While driving:** `me.position` follows the driver's seat, so the minimap, zones, the objective beacon and multiplayer all keep working unchanged. Needs drain as when idle (`moving` false). Gigs: delivery drop-offs need E at the door, so you must get out; driving to a drop-off is allowed (anti-exploit tuning per job stays with each job, DESIGN 3). R (reset) gets you out and sends you to the spawn; the vehicle stays where it was.
- **Camera:** a chase camera behind and above (pickup 8 m back, 3.2 m up; flatbed 11 m, 4.0 m), aimed 1.5 m above the vehicle and 4 m ahead, following its yaw with a lag (1 − e^(−4 dt)); the sightline fade targets the vehicle. Zoom wheel within ±30%.
- **0.33 vs 0.35:** **0.33:** driving, enter/exit, collisions, camera, parking data, saved vehicle poses (Q2). **0.35:** fuel (tank, pumps, can, low-fuel warning, tow), cargo and load (the flatbed's 8 m/s), selling planks, and the **multiplayer vehicle pose relay** (an optional `vehicle:{model, yaw}` on the relay's position message, drawn as the vehicle on other screens). Until then, remote players see a driver as their avatar gliding at vehicle speed (known, documented limitation).

**Decided (Q1: split).** Three heights exist today: the **terrain elevation** (0 everywhere), the **surface footing** (`footingAt`: per-surface layer offsets, pavement +0.165, road −0.035, grass −0.095; cosmetic layering, not terrain) and **decks** (`world.floorAt`: the sawmill's 0.30 and 0.20 m floors). The proposal splits them:
- `heightAt(x, z)` in a new pure module `dist/height.js`: **the terrain elevation, 0 today**. No dependency on the world, so the terrain mesh, tree layout, saves and tests can call it before the world exists (later it reads `MAP.relief`).
- `world.standHeight(x, z)` = `heightAt(x, z) + (floorAt(x, z) ?? footingAt(x, z))`: what the player's feet, put-down logs and planks use. Vehicles use `heightAt + FOOTING[surface]` (never a deck). Deck heights become **relative to the ground under them** (today identical, since the ground is 0).
- Call sites converted in 0.33: player feet (`frame()`), vehicles, landmark placement (`world.js` sets y = 0 today; changes to `heightAt`), the Eastbank prop chunks, generated trees and stumps, ground logs and plank drops. Interior floors keep their own heights.
- **Test:** `height.js` has a test-only override (`setHeightFunction`); with the terrain raised by +5 m, the player, a vehicle, a landmark, a tree and a put-down plank stack all stand 5 m higher, and nothing else moves. This replaces "a test fails if gameplay code hardcodes ground height" with a behavioural check (grepping for `y = 0` would be brittle).
- Plots, building footprints, lots and yards get `pad: true` in data (flat pads for later terrain carving); validateMap checks that every building plot has it.
- **User's reading:** one `heightAt(x, z)` that includes `floorAt` and is flat elsewhere. The difference: in that version `heightAt` depends on the built world (landmarks) and on the surface footing, so the later terrain mesh and tree layout could not call it, and decks would stay absolute. If the single function is preferred, it is easy to build as `heightAt = elevation + (floorAt ?? footing)`, with the elevation part kept internal.

#### I. Refuelling (design only: to be built after driving, with the 0.35 fuel system; nothing here is in the game yet)
*Drafted by Sonnet 5.5 at the user's request (the brief asked for the Opus pass; an Opus review of this section is recommended before it is built).* Models: the six `prop.*` gas-station props (ASSETS.md); anchors come from `propInfo(id).anchors`. Blender +Y is game -z: a `py` face looks toward -z, an `ny` face toward +z.
1. **Taking the nozzle.** Interaction candidate "E Take the diesel nozzle (2.10 $/L)" when the player is within 0.9 m of a face's `anchor_use` point (distance on the ground; the nearest face wins, so the two sides of one island never compete: they are 2.6 m apart). The holster nozzle (an instance of `prop.fuel-nozzle[-diesel]`, hidden by a zero-scale matrix) leaves; a second copy of the nozzle mesh is parented to the right hand, grip centre on the hand (the file's origin), spout forward. **Arm pose:** one arm held out in front, like the log's one-handed pose but lower: one more entry in the arm-pose table in `chop-path.js` (where CARRY lives) with its own blend; the log and plank poses are not edited (known open item: carry pose in real play).
2. **Hose** (all runtime, always, so a pump never looks unfinished). One tube builder: 10 segments x 6 sides = 66 vertices, **120 triangles**, radius 0.02 m, the existing dark flat material (a `MeshStandardMaterial` of one dark colour, shared with the other dark props; no new material). **Idle:** hose from `anchor_hose` to `anchor_nozzle` (about 0.7 m apart), sagging 0.1 m; built once when the forecourt is created, merged with the others into **one static mesh per forecourt** (8 hoses = 960 triangles, 1 draw call, zero per-frame work). **In use:** one live hose mesh for the whole game (66 vertices rewritten per frame, about 800 bytes of upload, well under 0.05 ms): from the pump's `anchor_hose` to the right-hand position, sagging in the middle (sag = 0.12 x current length), every point kept at least 0.03 m above `standHeight` (section E) so it never sinks into the paving. **Maximum length 3.5 m** straight line from the outlet to the hand: at 3.0 m the hose is taut and a toast says "Hose at its limit"; past 3.5 m the nozzle returns to the holster (the live hose is replaced by the idle one). 5. **Returning.** E at the pump (within 0.9 m of the face it was taken from) puts the nozzle back. A request larger than the room or the cash is clipped to what fits and is paid (down to a whole step), never refused partway; it errors only when nothing can be sold. 8. **Interactions.** `actions.js` gets one more kind, `'fuel'`, true while E is held and the pump is flowing: filling then blocks every other action and every other action blocks filling (the existing rule, `canStart`). ### 4.15 Careers and the Lumberjack company (0.35 design, Opus 5.5, 2026-11-05; numbers are placeholders for Haiku to tune)
**Goal:** the first job that works end to end and stays interesting for many hours: a career inside a company, with ranks, a daily set of varied tasks, and new tools, vehicles and buildings opened by rank. Not a single repeated loop.

**Decided with the user (2026-11-05):** company first, freelance later (Pine Ridge belongs to the company; logs and planks only pay on shift until a late rank opens a timber lease); the office goes on plot I5 (replacing the hut beside the plank dock) and the Eastbank Warehouse (I1) becomes the company's lumber warehouse, both with interiors; the axe ladder ships in 0.35, wear and repairs stay in 0.37; daylight is 6 AM to 6 PM; clocking in is done on the phone; the flatbed can be bought at Lumberjack level 5; chainsaws are sold only by the company; axes are sold in stores and on the phone.

#### A. Employers (generic; every later job uses the same shape)

- **Every career starts at the bottom.** You are hired in person: the first visit to the owner in the office is a short interview (dialog), which hires you as rank 1. Later visits are for promotions. Everything else (clock in, tasks, pay) is on the phone.

#### B. Ranks (Ridgeline; each rank adds tasks, tools and access, and keeps the earlier ones)

|---|---|---|---|
| 1 Trainee | hired | fell and buck marked pines, carry logs to the landing; sawmill access (none before hiring) | Axe (tier 1), Hand Saw |

Retune so L5 is reached in about 3 shifts, L10 in about 12 and L20 in about 40 (a shift is 12 game hours = 24 real minutes). Today's curve (40 XP to L2, +20 per level) stays; only the XP per action changes.

#### C. Shifts and pay
- **Clock in** from the phone's Job app between 6:00 AM and 5:30 PM (at least half an hour left). The shift ends at **6:00 PM** at the latest (automatic clock-out with a toast); you can clock out earlier. One shift per game day.
- **Wage** per game hour by rank (Trainee $8, +$2 per rank, placeholder), counted only while you are **on site** (inside a site area or in a company vehicle). Off site the Job app shows "Off site · not paid"; after 60 game minutes off site you are clocked out. Travel to work is unpaid (clock in when you arrive, or pay the walk).
- **Piece pay** on top: per log at the landing ($1.00), per plank milled ($0.25), per load hauled to Eastbank ($15), per rack stacked, per order filled (placeholders). Pay is the wage plus piece pay; it is paid in cash at clock-out, with a **pay stub** (hours, pieces, bonus) kept in the Job app (last 7).
- **Daily tasks:** at clock-in the company hands out 2-4 tasks from the rank's pool, chosen by counters (not random, so a save is repeatable) and varied day to day, e.g. "Fell 6 marked pines in block C", "Mill 30 planks", "Haul one full load to the timber yard", "Stack 40 planks in rack 2", "Deliver 20 planks to 3 Cedar Loop by 2 PM". ### 4.16 Lumber warehouse, order tickets and the forklift (0.35 step 8; design by Opus 5.5, 2026-11-06; decided with the user where marked)
**Goal:** the last rank-4 and rank-5 work of the Ridgeline career: stack planks in racks, pick customer orders, load them for pickup or deliver them. It reuses the systems that exist (company stock, the customer truck, `notifyPhone`, the Jobs app, the tracker, cargo on beds) instead of adding new ones.

#### A. How an order reaches the player (decided with the user)
- **Notifications:** while clocked in at Ridgeline at rank 4 (Yard hand) or higher, new order tickets come in during the shift. Each one shakes the phone and pings: "New order #14 · Greenway Builders · 24 planks · pickup by 2:00 PM".
- **Phone, Jobs, Shift:** the open tickets are listed (customer, what, deadline, pickup or delivery). The phone only shows them; it does not take them.
- **The ticket table in the warehouse:** E at the order table takes a ticket (a ticket printer on the table). Taking it makes it a tracker task with its steps. You are never given work while you are across the map.
- **Old tickets (decided):** tickets nobody took expire when the warehouse closes (6 PM), with no penalty. A taken **pickup** ticket is due the same day (it fails at its deadline or at clock-out). A taken **delivery** ticket (rank 5) has a 1 or 2 day deadline and carries over to the next shift. At most 3 taken tickets at once. A failed ticket pays nothing and costs reputation (placeholder -3).

#### B. Tickets (`tickets.js`, pure; not `orders.js`, which is the phone Store)
- Ticket: `{id, customer, kind:'pickup'|'delivery', planks, issuedAt, dueAt, status:'open'|'taken'|'staged'|'done'|'failed', staged, dest}` (dest: a delivery's home or build site from the map).
- **Release by counters, never random:** each game day has a fixed set of release times (placeholder: 7:00, 10:00, 13:00 for pickups; one delivery at 9:00 from rank 5), and the day number and the shifts worked pick the customer, the size (12, 24, 40 planks) and the deadline. A save always gets the same tickets.
- Customers are a short list of named businesses and residents (placeholders: Greenway Builders, Cedar Heights Homes, Alder Hardware, the resident of a Cedar Loop house); delivery destinations are house plots and one build site.
- Saved on the company: `progress.companies.ridgeline.tickets = {nextId, list}` (at most 12, finished ones pruned at the day change), so a future owner inherits the open orders (owner orders later read and write the same list).
- Pay: a picked and shipped pickup order pays the `orders` piece ($8.00); a delivered order pays the `deliveries` piece ($25.00); both add reputation (+1). Placeholders; they sit in `PIECE_PAY`.

#### C. The warehouse (I1, the Eastbank Warehouse, `industrial-q` at x 163, z 38.6, facing north)
**Updated 2026-11-06 after the first look (the user: it must look like a warehouse, much bigger; the upper shelves are forklift-only):** the hall is **36 x 24 x 7 m** (`warehouse-layout.js` is the plan: `HALL`, `UNITS`, `FACE_ROWS`), with three shelving units of eight bays, **five faces A to E, three levels, 120 cells** of up to 40 planks (ids like `B4-2`: face B, bay 4, level 2), 4 m aisles (a forklift turns in 3.5 m), slatted decks, level 1 on the floor and reachable by hand, levels 2 and 3 for the forklift (`reachableByHand`; `stackPlanks` refuses them by hand). Front zone (z 6.8 to 12): the person door in the middle, the dock's roller door and the delivery pile at the front right, the order table and lockers at the front left, three staging pallets between, a painted forklift bay at the left. Each shelving unit is its own set of instanced meshes and fades like a wall when it stands between the camera and you; its planks fade with it. The older numbers below (14 x 10 m, twelve bays A1..C4) are superseded.
- **Getting in:** a person door beside the one loading door (E, 6 AM to 6 PM, like the office). Inside is an interior scene built far from the map (like `office.js`), sized from the model's footprint.
- **Inside:** numbered racks along the back and sides (A1 to C4: 12 slots, each holds up to 40 planks), a **receiving** area by the loading door, the **order table** with the ticket printer, and a **staging lane** of 3 pallets by the loading door (one per taken ticket).
- **Stock:** the company's planks become two places: the lumberyard pallets outside (what you deliver, as now) and the racks (`companies.ridgeline.racks = {A1: planks, ...}`). Orders are picked from the racks only.
- **Stack racks (rank 4, the `stack` task, "Stack N racks"):** carry planks from receiving (fed by the lumberyard stock) and put them in a rack slot; a slot filled to 40 counts as a rack ($5.00).
- **Pick an order (rank 4, the `pick` task):** with a taken ticket, E at a rack slot takes up to 4 planks in your hands for that ticket (the tracker names the slot that has them), E at the ticket's staging pallet puts them down. Fully staged, the ticket is `staged`.
- **Pickup:** when a pickup ticket is staged, the customer truck (`customer-truck.js`) drives in and backs onto the warehouse's out-bay (`warehouse-out`). E at the loading door inside ("Load order #14 onto the truck") moves the staged planks onto its bed (drawn as boards), the truck leaves, and the ticket is done. The truck is outside and you are inside: nothing crosses scenes except the cargo numbers.
- **Delivery (rank 5, the `deliver` task):** a staged delivery ticket is loaded through the loading door onto your flatbed (company or your own) parked at the out-bay, driven to its destination, and E at the drop spot unloads it. It reuses cargo on beds and the drop spots of houses.

- **Where it drives:** only inside the warehouse interior (its own small loop with the interior's `blocked()`, using `vehicle.js`'s arcade step), never on the roads. Top speed 3 m/s. #### I. Pallets, the supply truck and put-away (design by Opus 5.5, 2026-11-06; decided with the user)
This replaces plank counts in the warehouse with pallet objects, and it replaces the instant crew delivery (`supply.js`, H) with a truck you can see.

**Pallets are objects.**
- A pallet is `{id, product, count, at}`. `product` is `'planks'` today; other products later. `count` is at most 40. `at` is one of:
  - `{rack: 'B4-2'}`: a rack cell (one pallet per cell);
  - `{fork: true}`: on the forklift's forks;
  - `{inbound: 0..5}`: an outdoor staging spot;
  - `{staging: 0..2}`: an order's staging square inside;
  - `{floor: {x, z, yaw}}`: set down anywhere on the warehouse floor;
  - `{truck: uid}`: on a vehicle's bed.
- Saved as `companies.ridgeline.pallets` (at most 200). On load, the old `racks` counts become one pallet per cell and `forkload` becomes a pallet on the forks; both old fields are then dropped. This adds a field and converts old data, so no schema bump is needed.
- **An empty pallet disappears** (the crew collects the empties). This applies to a rack pallet picked empty by hand and to a staged order's pallet once it ships.
- **The inside delivery pile is removed** (decided). `stock.planks` is the **lumberyard's** stock again: what the sawmill and players produce at the dock. The supply truck draws from it.
- **Picking by hand stays:** E at a floor-level rack pallet takes a stack of 4; E at an order's staging square puts it on that order's pallet (the pallet is created when the first stack goes down).

**Every pallet shows what it holds (decided).**
- A small tag above the pallet shows the product icon and the count (for example, a plank icon and "40").
- Tags are drawn only within 10 m, for the nearest 24 pallets. They are **one InstancedMesh** with an icon-and-number atlas (one draw call, not a sprite per pallet).
- The prompt line also names the pallet the forks or your hands are at: "Pallet · 40 planks · rack B4-2".

**Physical forks (decided: no E shortcut).** The rules are pure, in `forklift.js`:
- **Pocket:** a pallet is 1.2 m deep, 1.0 m wide and 0.14 m high, with a fork pocket from 0.03 to 0.11 m above its base. The tines can enter only when:
  - their top is inside the pocket band;
  - they are within 0.08 m of the pallet's centre line, sideways;
  - the forklift is within 6° of square to the pallet.

  Otherwise a pallet is solid to the tines: the forklift stops, and the prompt says why ("Forks too high", "Line up with the pallet").
- **Engaged:** the tines are in at least 0.6 m. Raising the forks then lifts the pallet (`at` becomes `{fork: true}`), and it rides with the carriage. Backing out without raising simply leaves the pallet where it is.
- **Setting down:** lowering a carried pallet stops when its base meets a surface.
  - Over a rack cell at deck height, within 0.15 m and 8° of the cell, it snaps square into the cell. Outside that, the forks will not lower further ("Line up with the bay").
  - Over a staging square, it snaps to the square.
  - Anywhere else on the floor, it stays where it is (`floor`).
- **Over-full staging:** if a pallet set on an order's staging square holds more than the order needs, the yard crew splits it at once. The order keeps what it needs; the rest becomes a **surplus pallet** on the nearest inbound spot (inside, see put-away) and joins the put-away queue.
- **One pallet on the forks at a time.** Two pallets cannot share a cell or square.

**The supply truck (decided: visible; a player may drive it, an NPC otherwise).**
- When orders need planks the warehouse does not have (the `supply.js` shortfall, now counting pallets everywhere, plus pallets on their way), a **supply run** is created: 1 to 3 pallets of 40.
- **Who drives it:**
  - It is offered for 30 game minutes as an optional job (decided) to a Sawyer (rank 5) on shift, on the order table and the phone.
  - Taking it means driving the Ridgeline flatbed (or your own truck with a bed) to the lumberyard. E at the lumberyard stack loads pallets from its stock. Then you drive to the outdoor staging and unload with E. It pays a piece (`supply`) and must be done within 2 game hours, or it fails like a delivery and falls back to the NPC.
  - If nobody takes it, an NPC flatbed does the run (`customer-truck.js` routes).
  - In co-op, another player on that shift can take it (4.17).
- **Where the planks come from:** the lumberyard stock first. When it is empty, the NPC truck brings them from a mill off the map, entering from the map edge like the customer truck. A player run is offered only when the lumberyard has the planks.
- **Unloading:** the truck parks alongside the **outdoor staging**: 6 painted pallet spots beside the warehouse's roller door. The exact place is chosen in the build step, clear of the customer truck's route and the dock bay. Its pallets go onto free spots one every 3 seconds, sliding off the bed (no NPC forklift).
- **If the outdoor staging is full,** the run waits and no truck comes until a spot frees (decided). Pallets on the outdoor staging count as held, so a full staging never orders more.

**Put-away (decided: a player first, the NPC crew as the fallback).**
- Pallets on the outdoor staging (and surplus pallets inside) go into the racks.
- **A Yard hand on shift gets the task** "Put away N pallets", paying a piece (`putaway`) per pallet. They do it with the forklift, which drives out through the roller door (below).
- **If no Yard hand is on shift,** or a pallet has waited 30 game minutes, the crew moves one pallet every 5 game minutes. The pallet disappears from its spot and appears in the **most sensible free cell**: the free cell nearest the roller door at level 1 (reachable by hand), then level 2, then level 3. Players may put pallets anywhere they like.
- **When every rack cell is full** (120 cells, 4,800 planks: hard to reach), put-away stops, the outdoor staging stays full, and no new supply comes (decided).

**The roller door is a drive-through for the forklift.** This is needed for put-away now and for loading larger trucks later (4.17 K).
- Interiors are separate scenes, so driving up to the open roller door from inside fades you outside, at the door, on the same forklift (and the other way).
- The forklift's state gains `where: 'inside' | 'outside'`. Outside it uses the outdoor collision and `heightAt` (flat there), stays within Eastbank Works (it refuses to leave the paving: "The forklift stays in the yard"), and is drawn in the outdoor scene.
- **Performance:** +14,000 triangles outdoors, drawn only within 60 m of it, and loaded lazily when you first go to Eastbank. That is inside the budget.

**Seam and co-op:** every pallet change is an `applyCompany` action (`palletLift`, `palletSet`, `pick`, `stage`, `supplyTake`, `supplyLoad`, `supplyUnload`, `putaway`, `crewTick`), so the co-op server (4.17) runs the same rules. A pallet on one forklift's forks is locked to that driver.

### 4.17 Co-op server update (design by Opus 5.5, 2026-11-06; decided with the user where marked; numbers are placeholders for Haiku to tune)
Goal: two to four players (eight at most) share one world. What one player sees, the others see. Single player stays as it is: fully offline, local save.

#### A. Two kinds of lobby (decided)
- **World lobby (the default):** the world is stored on the server, owned by the account that created it, joined with its code. It runs only while someone is connected: the first player to join wakes it and the clock moves; when the last one leaves it is saved and frozen (no clock, no tickets). It is never wiped for being empty. Placeholder: three worlds per account.
- **Host lobby:** a player opens their own game to others. **The host's save is the world**, and the authority runs in the host's browser (the same pure code the server would run). The relay only forwards. When the host leaves, the lobby closes.
- In both kinds **everything in the world is shared** (decided): an open server is always shared.
- **Settings, chosen by whoever creates the lobby** (decided: the host decides), shown to everyone who joins:
  - **Characters:** *Bring your own* (your character, money, inventory, skills, career and vehicles come from your own save, and changes are written back to it) or *Made in this world* (each player starts a new character inside the world; the world stores it under their account). This is one switch for money, inventory and career together, because mixing them (for example your own money with a world career) opens duplication exploits.
  - **Max players:** 2 to 8 (decided). Above 4 the menu warns: "Designed for 4 players; more may be slow."
  - **Sleep:** time skips only when everyone is in bed (default), or never.
- Sign-in: a Google account is required to create or join a World lobby (decided; more sign-in options later). Host lobbies need it too, because the player id has to be stable for pay and ownership.
- `server:lan` (the local Node relay) stays positions-only in this update. Because the authority is pure, it can later run the World code in Node with a file store.

#### B. What is world state and what is player state
- **World (one copy, held by the authority):** the clock (day, minutes); companies (tickets, racks, yard stock, the forklift's load, cut blocks and marks); forest plots (damage, felled, stump, cleared, sapling, regrowth); logs on the ground, plank drops, the sawmill belt; **every vehicle** (owner, model, colour, pose, fuel, cargo, tailgate, who sits in it); the customer and dock trucks (derived from tickets); house ownership and rent; the dealership's stock; parcels waiting at houses.
- **Player:** character, money, inventory, vitals, career (rank, XP, reputation), shift, pay stubs, orders, what you carry (a log on the shoulder, planks in the hands), the apartment.
- With *Bring your own*, player state lives in the player's save and the server **trusts** it, because a save can be edited; the lobby is labelled "trusted (friends)". With *Made in this world*, the world holds player state too and the server is fully authoritative.
- The world is a set of **keyed documents** (`clock`, `company:ridgeline`, `plot:<key>`, `log:<id>`, `vehicle:<uid>`, `property:<id>`, `drops`). Each is small. A change replaces whole documents, which keeps the wire format and storage simple.

#### C. The authority and the protocol (PROTOCOL 2)
- `dist/authority.js` (pure, new): `createAuthority({world, rules})` with `act(playerId, action, now) -> {ok|error, changed: [docs], effects: [{playerId, money, piece, rep, item...}]}`. It runs in the World Durable Object and in the host's browser, and its tests run in Node. It never touches the DOM, Three.js or `Date`.
- Messages (on top of the existing hello and positions): `snapshot {rev, docs}` on join; `act {seq, action}` from a client; `result {seq, ok|error}` back to that client; `patch {rev, docs}` broadcast to everyone, including the actor. A client that sees a gap in `rev` asks for a new snapshot.
- **Discrete actions wait for the answer** (an E press, a ticket, a sale): nothing changes on screen until `result` arrives, usually within 50 to 150 ms. There is no prediction and no rollback, so a refusal is just a toast.
- **Continuous things are streams, not actions:** your walking position (as today) and the pose of the vehicle or forklift you sit in. The driver streams the pose; the authority keeps the latest and saves it when you get out.
- The relay's position stream drops from 20 to 10 a second, and nothing is sent while standing still (see cost, section I). Other clients interpolate between updates.

#### D. The seam: every world change goes through `applyWorld(action)`
- `applyCompany` (4.16 G) becomes `applyWorld`. Offline it runs the rule on the local save at once, as today. In a lobby it sends `act` and applies the result.
- **Actions to move onto the seam (the largest part of the build):**
  - tickets (done);
  - the warehouse: `stackPlanks`, `takeReceiving`, `takeFromRack`, `forkTake`, `forkPut`, `forkTakePile`, forklift enter and exit;
  - the forest: a chop hit, felling, bucking, picking up and dropping a log, gather, `clearStump`, `plantSapling`, the day's marks;
  - the sawmill (belt in, planks out) and plank drops;
  - vehicles: enter and exit (seat lock), buy on the lot, delivery, fuel fill, can pour, cargo add and remove, the tailgate;
  - the dock sale and company stock;
  - houses: rent, buy, sell (section H).
- Every rule takes `playerId` and `now` in the action and reads no globals, so the server can run the same code. A test imports every rule module with no DOM and fails if one reaches for `window`, `document`, `Date.now` or `Math.random`. Randomness comes from a seed in the action.
- Chop hits are frequent (a few a second at most), so they are sent one by one. They are rate-limited, not batched.

#### E. Conflicts
- **The first to reach the authority wins.** The loser gets a refusal ("Sam got there first") and nothing changes. This applies to a tree, a log, a plank stack, a rack cell, a ticket, a vehicle seat and the forklift.
- **Locks:** a taken ticket is locked to its taker ("Taken by Sam"), but anyone on a Ridgeline shift may pick and stage for it (decided in 4.16 G). A seat belongs to whoever sits in it until they get out. Only a vehicle's owner can drive it; others can ride later, not in this update. Anything you carry is player state, so nobody else can take it.

#### F. Pay and anti-cheat
- **Each player is paid their own pieces and wage. The order pay goes to whoever loads or delivers.**
- *Made in this world:* the authority pays. The wage is counted from the server clock and the player's last streamed position (on site or not). Pieces come only from actions the authority ran. Checks: **reach** (the player's last position is within 4 m of the target, 6 m for vehicles), **rank** (from the world's copy), and **rate** (per action type, for example 4 chop hits a second).
- *Bring your own:* the effects are sent to the player's client, which applies them to its own save. The same reach and rate checks run, but money and rank cannot be verified, which is why the lobby is labelled trusted.
- **Money moving between players** (a house sold from one player to another) is only allowed with *Made in this world*, or between two trusted players who both accept.

#### G. Joining and dropping
- **Join:** snapshot, then the world's clock. A *Bring your own* player keeps their own clock in single player only; in the lobby the world's clock rules.
- **Drop (connection lost):** what you carried falls at your feet as world objects. Your seat and the forklift are freed (the load stays on the forks). A vehicle stays parked where it stopped. Your taken tickets are held for 15 real minutes for a rejoin (the relay already retries at 2, 5 and 10 s). After that they go back on the board **without** the reputation penalty. A failure from a missed deadline still counts.
- **Shift:** it continues across a rejoin; the existing hour-off-site rule clocks you out if you stay away. Leaving the lobby on purpose clocks you out.
- **The world's clock:** 30 game minutes per real minute while anyone is present. Sleep skips to 6 AM only when everyone is in bed (the setting above), and runs rent, bills and deliveries for every player.

#### H. Houses (decided: a bought house is the buyer's)
- Ownership is world state: `property:<id>.owner`. Rent works the same way, and one house has one tenant or owner.
- **Selling:** sell back to the world for 80% of the price paid (placeholder), or list it for a player at a price you set. The buyer accepts while both are online, and money and deed swap in one authority action.
- **Visiting other players' houses (recommended yes):** the owner sets a door rule: *Locked* (default), *Knock* (E at the door sends the owner a phone prompt; Accept lets you in) or *Open*. Only the owner moves furniture. Apartments stay personal and instanced (room `apartment:<playerId>`), so everyone can have the same apartment, and nobody can visit them (decided).
- Visiting reuses the existing `house:<id>` rooms. Shared furniture layouts become a world document per house.

#### I. Cost (decided: stay on the free plan as long as possible)
- The free plan allows, per day: 100,000 Durable Object requests (incoming WebSocket messages count 20 to 1), 13,000 GB-s of duration (128 MB billed while awake), 100,000 SQLite row writes, and 5 GB stored.
- **Estimate for a 4-player lobby, 1 hour:** about 40 position messages a second in, about 7,200 billed requests, about 450 GB-s. **The free plan holds about 14 to 25 lobby-hours a day in total, across all players** (requests run out first at 20 positions a second; at 10 a second and nothing while standing still, duration runs out first, at about 28 hours). When a limit is reached, new actions fail until 00:00 UTC, so the game must say "the server is busy today" instead of breaking.
- **Storage writes:** changed documents are kept in memory and written every 10 s, and when the last player leaves. That is about 1,500 row writes per lobby-hour, comfortably inside the limit.
- **For a public release:** the Workers Paid plan ($5 a month, more included usage, pay as you go beyond it) is needed. The estimate is about a cent or less per 4-player lobby-hour; check the pricing page at the time. A Host lobby costs the same relay traffic as a World lobby; only the storage is saved. A peer-to-peer (WebRTC) Host lobby would cost almost nothing but is a separate project; note it and do not build it now.

#### J. Tickets: random and scaled (decided: no fixed number)
- This replaces the fixed 7/9/10/13 releases in `tickets.js`. Every game hour from 7:00 to 16:00 a seeded roll (seed: world id + day + hour, so the server and tests agree) releases tickets.
- Expected per hour: 0.4 + 0.35 × the eligible players on shift (Yard hand or higher). A quarter are deliveries.
- Open (untaken) tickets are capped at 2 + 2 × the eligible players, so a busy crew always has work and a lone player is not buried.
- The same rule runs in single player with one player. All numbers are placeholders for Haiku.
